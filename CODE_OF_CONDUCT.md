# Code of conduct

## The short version

Be decent to people. Assume the person you are replying to is acting in good
faith and knows something you do not.

## What is expected

- Give feedback on the work, not the person. "This drops the focus ring" is
  useful; "this is careless" is not.
- Accept that a maintainer may decline a contribution. A design system stays
  coherent by saying no to good ideas that do not fit, and that is not a
  judgement of the person who proposed one.
- Take the disagreement to the reasoning. Most arguments here should end with
  someone pointing at a WCAG criterion, a measurement, or a test.

## What is not acceptable

Harassment, discriminatory language, personal attacks, sustained disruption,
publishing someone's private information, and unwelcome sexual attention. This
applies in issues, pull requests, commit messages, and anywhere else the project
is discussed.

## Reporting

Report a problem to the maintainers privately, through a GitHub issue marked for
maintainer attention or by contacting the `cordlybot` organisation owners
directly. Reports are handled confidentially.

Maintainers may edit, hide, or delete contributions that break this document, and
may block accounts for repeated or severe behaviour. Where a maintainer is the
subject of a report, the remaining maintainers handle it and that maintainer
takes no part in the decision.

## Scope

This applies in all project spaces, and when someone is representing the project
in public.
